## Serology
Antibodies, also known as immunoglobulins (Ig), are produced by the immune system in response to exposure to antigens in the form of infections or vaccinations. 
- there are 5 different types of immunoglobulins in humans
- IgM produces first but quickly wanes after the infection is cleared
- IgG is the longest lasting isotype

types of antibody presence meaning (Metcalf paper)

Serology, what it is, and what it can tell us  
- can be used as a tool to provide longer-term view of exposure than current diagnostic tests. The application of serology is becoming increasingly recognised as a useful tool to complement
traditional epidemiological and surveillance methods.

## Applications of multiplex serology
There are many applications of multiplex multi-pathogen serology from mapping prevalence, to evaluating interventions, to understanding immune dynamics across populations. This section will highlight a few publications show casing the use of multi-pathogen multiplex serology.

1. [Estimate seroprevalence of NTDs in Malaysia](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.924316/full)  
Multiplex serology has been used to establish the prevalence of 6 neglected tropical diseases in Malaysia. When combining the serology with survey information, the researchers found risk factors associated with each pathogen. They noted that across the pathogens, age was the most consistent risk factor, with exposure generally increasing over time. Socio-economic status was also important, as individuals with higher wealth tended to have lower exposure for several diseases. Risk also varied by ethnicity, gender, and certain behaviors, such as going to the forest. Geostatistics maps were produced to show the sero-prevalence of each antigen.  

2. [Evaluate the impact of bed net distribution on burden of lymphatic filariasis and human malaria in Mozambique](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0006278)  
Multi-pathogen multiplex serology has been used to assess the impact of bed nets distribution on malaria and lymphatic filariasis in Mozambique. In this example, two cross-sectional household surveys were conducted with both collecting dried blood spots before bed net distribution and one year later. The bed net campaign did not achieve enough coverage to decrease P. falciparum Rapid Diagnostic Test positivity. However, serological analysis revealed notable intervention impacts. Using multiplex serology, community bed net use was associated with reduced seropositivity for two malaria antigens. Additionally, there were significant decreases in P. malariae antigen seroconversion rates. This illustrates the enhanced sensitivity of multiplex serological approaches for intervention evaluation.  

3. [Identify geographic factors of malarial focal spots in very low transmission settings in the Amazon](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-52239-5)  
In the Peruvian Amazon, multiplex serology confirmed extremely low malaria transmission in a setting where microscopy and PCR detected no active infections. Despite this, low but measurable seropositivity revealed ongoing exposure patterns, particularly in older individuals and in villages farthest from health facilities. Spatial analyses identified hot spots of transmission in remote areas.  

4. [Identify immunological interactions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394338266_Assessing_the_effect_of_malaria_exposure_history_on_tetanus_antibody_waning_rates_among_children_in_Jinja_and_Tororo_Districts_Uganda)  
Multiplex serology has also been used to study immune interactions. In Uganda, antibodies to the tetanus toxoid waned faster in children living in high-malaria transmission areas, suggesting that malaria exposure may influence vaccine-derived immunity and potentially inform booster timing.  

5. [Assess immunity gaps](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65957-9)  
In a post-pandemic respiratory study, young children experienced larger drops in antibody levels to multiple respiratory diseases during COVID-19 restrictions and showed faster waning across multiple respiratory pathogens, demonstrating how multiplex platforms can assess population-level immunity debt.
